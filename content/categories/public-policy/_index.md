These articles are part of a continuing series that looks at how American
government and the economy are organized, and at changes that would make them
work better for **the people** they are supposed to serve. Each stands on its
own, but they are strongly related to each other: how we choose
representatives, how money bends that process, how the courts are chosen and
held accountable, how economic power is checked, and how healthcare could be
organized. The theme of this site applies to all of them: the solutions are
known, and what is usually missing is the will to use them. Another consistent
theme is that **people** matter. Systems, organizations, and polices are built
by and for **people** and only have value in that context.

You may not agree with things written here. If you believe you have an
improvement either substantive or grammatical that I've written about you are
encouraged to suggest the improvement as described in {{ relref about }}. I may
not agree with you. You are free to publish your own thoughts on your own site,
this is mine. If you disagree with my position, I encourage you to try and
change my mind. If you have a well supported argument, I will consider it.
                                                                                
***Content will change*** over time as solutions improve, evolve, or are found  
to be suboptimal.  So don't depend on something staying here forever.
                                                                                
Often improvements for public policy ills are blocked by entities that find the 
existing dysfunctional systems benefit them at the expense of the broader       
society. Consequently, often what is most needed is not naming the              
solution, but the political will to implement it.  I hope to some tiny extent   
articles here can increase the political pressure for positive change. If you   
desire policy solutions you see here, press your political representatives to   
make it happen and pay attention when you vote to which candidates are actually 
working to make positive solutions happen.
