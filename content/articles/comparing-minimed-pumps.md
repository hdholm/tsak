I have used MiniMed insulin pumps for many years. I recently upgraded from a
[MiniMed 780G](https://discourse.gohugo.io/t/enablegitinfo-docuMiniMed%20780Gmentation-and-behavior/6131/12)
 to a [MiniMed Flex](https://www.minimed.com/en-us/products/minimed-780g-insulin-pump-system).
Overall the Flex is an improvement, but there are some things I wish were
different.

Both systems pair with a continuous glucose monitor (CGM) and use its readings
to adjust basal insulin and deliver correction boluses automatically. The
algorithm is currently the same on both pumps, although I believe some future
algorithm updates may be available only for the Flex.

## Pros and cons of the Flex compared with the 780G

- **[+] Rechargeable.** The Flex charges on its own small MagSafe-style stand
  and needs about 30 minutes a week, instead of the AA batteries the 780G uses.
- **[+] Phone control.** It is controlled from a smartphone, so it is less
  obtrusive. You can wear it under clothing because you never need to touch it.
- **[−] Phone only.** The Flex can *only* be controlled from a smartphone. The
  pump itself shows nothing except LED color changes indicating the severity of
  alarms, and there is no way to bolus or suspend delivery without the phone.
  That can be a real problem in some environments.
- **[+] Smaller.** The Flex is somewhat smaller than the 780G, although I find
  the 780G small enough, and in use the Flex does not feel much smaller.
- **[−] Accessories.** The Flex is new. Although it is better suited to being
  worn under clothing, there are few good holsters or belt solutions yet. It
  ships with a belt clip, which by definition holds it over clothing rather
  than under it.

## The solutions are known

To stomp on the theme of this blog: put the Flex's technology (smartphone
control, recharging, updated hardware) into the 780G's form factor, and keep
the screen and buttons on the pump. That would have preserved compatibility
with existing belts and holsters, kept the freedom to operate the pump without
a phone, and retained essentially every other advantage of the Flex.

That was not the direction chosen. The Flex is a good pump, but so far it has
been only a marginal improvement over the 780G.
